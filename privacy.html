The short version

  • Doomky counts reels and Shorts on your phone. If you never sign in, your counts never leave your phone.
  • It only works inside Instagram and YouTube, and only looks at which screen is open. It cannot read your messages, captions, comments or anything you type, and it does not record your screen.
  • If you sign in, your handle, daily counts, friends and wars are stored on our server so the leaderboard and friends features work.
  • We collect crash reports and a short list of anonymous usage events to keep the app working. None of it includes what you watched.
  • No ads. We never sell your data. You can delete your account and its data in the app at any time.

2. The Accessibility service, in plain words

Doomky's main feature, counting the reels and Shorts you watch, needs Android's Accessibility service permission. You turn it on yourself in your phone's settings, after Doomky explains what it is for. Here is exactly what it does and does not do.

What it does

  • It is limited, in its configuration, to two apps: Instagram (com.instagram.android) and YouTube (com.google.android.youtube). It receives nothing from any other app.
  • Inside those two apps it reads screen layout information: which screen is open, whether that screen is Reels or Shorts, and when you move on to the next video. That is how each reel or Short is counted.
  • It shows a small count at the edge of your screen while you watch, a roast from the monkey, and a full-screen stop when you reach your daily limit. It can press "back" for you to leave the feed at your limit.

What it does not do

  • It does not read the text of messages, captions, comments, usernames, search terms or anything you type, including passwords.
  • It does not see what is in a video, take screenshots or record your screen.
  • It does not send anything it reads to us. What leaves the phone is only the numbers described below: how many reels and Shorts you watched, for how long, and whether Doomky was switched on.

You can switch the service off at any time in your phone's Accessibility settings. Doomky then stops counting.

3. What we collect and why

3.1 On your phone only

These stay on your device and are not sent to us:

  • Your reel and Short counts and time spent, per day and per app, and your streaks.
  • Your settings: daily limit, goal, language and any promise you made.
  • Pay a friend: if you use it, the friend's name, their UPI ID and the amount. Doomky only opens your own UPI app with those details filled in. You approve the payment in your UPI app with your own PIN. Doomky never handles, sees or confirms payments, and never sees your bank details.

Uninstalling Doomky deletes all of this from your phone.

3.2 If you sign in

Signing in is optional. Without it, the leaderboard, friends and wars do not work, but counting and the daily limit do. You can sign in with Google or with a sign-in link sent to your email address.

When you sign in with Google, Google shares your name, email address and profile picture with us. We keep only your email address. We do not store your Google name or profile picture.

3.3 Crash reports (everyone)

When the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a technical report so we can fix it. A report contains a stack trace (the names of the parts of the code involved, without error messages), the app version, your Android version and your device model, plus a random install ID.

Reports are processed by Sentry in its EU region. Sentry is set up to send no personal data: the user field is cleared before sending, IP addresses are not stored, and no screenshots or screen layouts are attached. A slimmer copy of the report (the install ID, app and Android version, device model and the top of the stack trace) is also saved on our Supabase server, in case Sentry is unavailable. Before it is saved, anything that looks like an email address or an account ID is removed.

3.4 Anonymous usage events (everyone)

To understand whether people can set the app up and keep using it, the app records a short, fixed list of events: first open, onboarding steps, turning on the Accessibility service, the first reel counted, the opening animation shown, signing in, adding a friend, starting a war, reaching your limit, sharing, and opening the app (at most once a day). Each event has a name, a time and the app version. Nothing about what you watched or what is on your screen is included.

Events are tied to a random install ID created by the app. It is not your advertising ID and is not shared with other apps. If you are signed in when a batch of events is sent, the server also links that batch to your account. Those linked events are deleted when you delete your account.

3.5 Push notifications

We are adding push notifications through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM), so that, for example, you can hear when a friend sends you a request or a war ends. To deliver them, your phone gets a device token from Firebase, which we store with your account. The token identifies the app install for delivery, not you by name. You can turn notifications off at any time in your phone's settings.

3.6 Camera

The friend code scanner uses your camera, only while the scanner is open and only after you allow it. Frames are processed on your phone, using Google Play services, to find a Doomky QR code. They are never saved or sent to us or anyone else. Reading a QR code from a screenshot also happens on your phone.

3.7 Settings from our server and update checks

When you open the app, it reads a small settings record from our server (for example, whether an update is needed or a notice should be shown), and every few hours it may check GitHub for a newer version. These requests do not send your counts or account details. As with any internet request, the servers involved see your IP address.

3.8 This website

This website has no analytics, ads or tracking cookies. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and fonts are loaded from Google Fonts. Like any web host, they may log your IP address and browser details for security and operation.

4. What we never collect

  • Your location.
  • Your contacts, photos, files or camera images.
  • Your messages, or the content of anything on your screen.
  • Anything from inside Instagram or YouTube beyond the daily counts above.
  • Financial or payment information.
  • Your advertising ID. Doomky has no ads and no advertising SDKs.

We do not sell your personal data, and we do not share it with anyone for their own advertising or marketing.

5. Who can see your information

  • The leaderboard: other signed-in Doomky users can see your handle and your reel count for today and this week.
  • Search and friend codes: signed-in users can find you by your handle or friend code and send you a friend request.
  • Friends: people you are friends with see your handle, your reel counts, whether Doomky is still reporting, and when you go over your limit.
  • Wars: people in a war with you see your handle, your totals during the war, and whether you are still reporting.
  • Nobody else sees your email address. Other users never see it.

You can remove or block someone at any time, and they stop seeing your details as a friend. Blocked people also stop seeing you on the leaderboard. Our database enforces these rules: each person can only read and change their own data, apart from the shared views above.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, for example in response to a valid order from a court or government authority in India, or to protect the safety of a person.

7. Legal basis

We only process personal data where the law allows it. Depending on where you live, we rely on:

  • Your consent (DPDP Act section 6; GDPR Article 6(1)(a)): turning on the Accessibility service, signing in, allowing the camera and allowing notifications. You can withdraw consent at any time by switching the permission off, signing out or deleting your account. Withdrawing does not affect processing that happened before.
  • Providing the service you asked for (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)): storing your account, counts, friends and wars so the leaderboard, friends and wars features work.
  • Legitimate interests (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), and under the DPDP Act the uses you agreed to when you started using the app: crash reports and anonymous usage events to keep the app stable and improve it, and rate limits, blocks and reports to prevent abuse. We have designed these to use as little data as possible and nothing that names you.
  • Legal obligations: where we must keep or disclose information by law.

9. Deleting your account

In Doomky, open Settings > Account > Delete account. This permanently deletes your account and the data linked to it from our servers straight away. If you can't use the app, email us instead. The full steps, and exactly what is deleted and kept, are on the delete your account page.

Signing out keeps your account but stops your phone sending anything to it.

11. India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

If you are in India, the DPDP Act gives you these rights as a Data Principal:

  • Access: a summary of the personal data we process about you, what we do with it, and who we have shared it with.
  • Correction and erasure: to correct, complete, update or erase your personal data.
  • Withdraw consent: as easily as you gave it. Switch off a permission, sign out, or delete your account.
  • Grievance redressal: to have a complaint answered by us.
  • Nominate: to name another person to use these rights for you if you die or become unable to.

Grievance officer: Shajith Bathina, shajith240@gmail.com. We will acknowledge your complaint within 7 days and aim to resolve it within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

12. EU and UK: GDPR

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, the GDPR (and the UK GDPR) gives you the right to access, correct and erase your data, to restrict or object to processing (including processing based on legitimate interests), to data portability (a copy of the data you gave us in a common, machine-readable format), and to withdraw consent at any time.

Doomky does not make decisions about you based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects. The leaderboard simply ranks reel counts.

You also have the right to complain to the data protection authority where you live or work. We would be grateful if you contacted us first, so we can try to fix it.

13. International transfers

We are based in India, and our providers run servers in several countries, including the United States and the European Union. This means your data may be processed outside the country where you live. Sentry processes crash reports in its EU region. Where data from the EEA or UK goes to a country without an adequacy decision, we rely on the Standard Contractual Clauses our providers offer in their data processing terms. Under the DPDP Act, personal data may be transferred outside India except to countries the Government of India restricts.

14. Children

Doomky is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. In India, the DPDP Act treats anyone under 18 as a child and requires a parent's verifiable consent before their personal data is processed. If you are under 18 in India, please only sign in with a parent's or guardian's permission. If you believe a child has given us personal data, email us and we will delete it.

15. Security

  • All data between the app and our servers is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).
  • Email sign-in links are secured so that only the phone that asked for the link can use it.
  • Our database uses row-level security: each person can only read and change their own records, apart from the shared leaderboard, friend and war views.
  • Database backups are encrypted with AES-256 before they are stored, and are deleted after 14 days.
  • Crash reports are stripped of personal details, and the server removes anything shaped like an email address or account ID before saving.

No system is perfectly secure. If a data breach affects you, we will tell you and the relevant authorities as the law requires, including the Data Protection Board of India.

16. Changes to this policy

We will update this policy when the app or the law changes. The "Last updated" date at the top will change. If a change materially affects how we use your data, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect and, where the law requires, ask for your consent again.
